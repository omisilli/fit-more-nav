A row of tabs that always fits. Narrow the window below — drag its right edge, or the slider: the tabs that no longer fit move to More, the lowest priority first, and come back as it grows. The open tab never leaves — pick one from More and it comes into the row while another goes. "More" is a dropdown, the tabs are targetFunctions; fit-more-nav only measures and decides. anchor sits beside them: read far down the long Overview tab, press another tab — in the row or in More — and the page comes back to the new tab's start.

Overview — a long tab

Everything at a glance. This tab has the highest priority: it is the last to leave.

Read on to the end of it, then press another tab — Contact in the row, or News from More. anchor brings the page back to the start of the tab you pressed, under the bar that followed you down.

These paragraphs only make the tab long. A page of terms or a long product description reads like this: section after section, the reader far below where the tab began by the time they reach the bottom.

Without anchor the new tab opens down here, at the height of the old one, and the reader lands in its middle — or below its end, looking at nothing.

With --pg-anchor-when: passed anchor only goes back up: pressed at the top of the tabs, or above them, nothing moves.

The copy of a tab in More carries the same data-pg-anchor as the tab, so it brings the page back the same way.
